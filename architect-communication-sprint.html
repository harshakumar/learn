Reading this sheet

Everything in orange is a placeholder — swap it for your real projects, numbers, and decisions. The dashed line above each conversation marks it as a template, not a script to memorize.

01 — Translate

Turn technical decisions into business language: cost, speed, risk.

02 — Structure

Context → Constraints → Options → Decision → Outcome, every time.

03 — Commit

State a point of view on trade-offs — don't just list pros and cons.

02

10-Day Plan

25–35 minutes a day. Record yourself where noted — hearing your own answer back is the fastest way to fix it.

DAY
1

Baseline + the Architect Answer Shape

Record yourself (2–3 min) answering "walk me through an architecture decision you made and why." Rewrite it using Context → Constraints → Options → Decision + why → Business outcome — the last step is what most engineers skip.

DAY
2

Translate Tech to Business Language

Take 5 past decisions. Write one sentence each translating them into cost, speed, risk, or scalability terms. Practice until they sound natural, not memorized.

DAY
3

Trade-offs Without Hedging

Pick 3 classic trade-offs in your domain. Practice a 30-second answer for each that states both sides, then commits: "here's when I'd choose each."

DAY
4

Handling Pushback

Have someone challenge one of your answers with "why not just do X instead?" Acknowledge genuinely, then explain the specific constraint that ruled it out. Watch your tone on playback.

DAY
5

Whiteboard / Think-Out-Loud

Narrate a system design out loud, stating assumptions explicitly before diving in ("I'm assuming read-heavy traffic here..."). This habit signals architect-level maturity on its own.

DAY
6

The Non-Technical Stakeholder Test

Explain one decision to someone with zero technical background. If they can repeat back roughly what and why, you passed. If they glaze over, simplify and retry.

DAY
7

Structuring a Design Discussion

Practice opening a mock review: Problem statement → success criteria → 2-3 options → recommendation → open questions for the room.

DAY
8

Full Mock Interview

20–30 min covering: background pitch, one architecture story, one trade-off, one pushback, one open-ended design prompt. Get feedback on pacing and confidence, not just content.

DAY
9

Executive Summary Drill

Compress your best story to 3 sentences (elevator, VP-length). Then expand it back to 2 minutes for a peer audience. Practice calibrating depth to the listener.

DAY
10

Full Rehearsal + Toolkit

One more mock interview, then build a one-page cheat sheet: pitch, 2 stories, 3 trade-off positions, 3 business-impact lines. Compare today's recording against Day 1.

20 Sample Conversations

Realistic short exchanges covering the situations architect interviews and the job itself actually throw at you. Replace every [bracketed] item with your own project, numbers, and reasoning.

1 — Architecture decision walkthroughINTERVIEW
INTERVIEWER

Walk me through an architecture decision you made and why.

YOU

At [company/project], we were facing [the problem] under constraints of [time/team size/budget]. I considered [option A] and [option B], and went with [option chosen] because [the deciding factor]. That decision translated to [business outcome — e.g. cut deploy time from X to Y].

2 — Business impact translationSTAKEHOLDER
STAKEHOLDER

Why should I care about this migration to [new system]?

YOU

Right now, [current pain point] is costing us [time/money/risk] every [week/release]. Moving to [new system] means [concrete business result] within [timeframe].

3 — Build vs. buy trade-offINTERVIEW
INTERVIEWER

When would you build something in-house versus buy a vendor solution?

YOU

If it's [core to our differentiation], I lean toward building — we did that with [example]. If it's [commodity capability] where a vendor has already solved it well, I buy — like we did with [example]. The deciding factor is usually [maintenance cost vs. strategic value].

4 — Monolith vs. microservicesINTERVIEW
INTERVIEWER

Monolith or microservices — what's your take?

YOU

Depends on team topology more than technology. At [X team size], we kept a monolith because [coordination cost of splitting outweighed the benefit]. Once we hit [trigger point — team size / scaling need], we split out [specific service] because [the specific reason].

5 — Handling "why not just use X"PUSHBACK
STAKEHOLDER

Why not just use [simpler alternative] instead?

YOU

That's a fair option, and it would work for [scenario where it's fine]. The reason it doesn't fit here is [specific constraint — scale, compliance, latency]. Given that, [your recommendation] holds up better long-term.

6 — Whiteboard system design openingDESIGN ROUND
INTERVIEWER

Design a system for [prompt].

YOU

Before I sketch anything — a few assumptions: I'm assuming [traffic pattern], [read/write ratio], and [scale target]. If any of those are off, tell me and I'll adjust. Starting from there, the core components are...

7 — Explaining to a non-technical stakeholderSTAKEHOLDER
STAKEHOLDER

What did the team actually do this quarter with the [project] work?

YOU

Think of it like [everyday analogy]. Before, [old painful state]. Now, [new state], which means [what changes for them].

8 — Opening a design review meetingMEETING
YOU

Quick framing before we dive in: the problem is [problem statement]. Success looks like [criteria]. I've got [2-3] options to walk through, and I'll flag the open questions I still want the room's input on at the end.

9 — Elevator pitch to a VPEXECUTIVE
VP

Give me the 30-second version of what you're proposing.

YOU

[Problem] is costing us [impact]. I'm proposing [solution], which gets us [outcome] by [timeframe], at a cost of roughly [cost/effort].

10 — Handling disagreement in a design meetingMEETING
PEER

I don't think that's the right approach — I'd rather we go with [alternative].

YOU

Say more about what's driving that — is it [concern A] or [concern B]? [Listen] That's a real trade-off. Given [constraint], here's why I still lean toward [your option] — but I'm open to revisiting if [condition changes].

11 — Justifying the cost of a decisionSTAKEHOLDER
STAKEHOLDER

This is going to take [X weeks/budget] — is it really worth it?

YOU

The upfront cost is [X], but the current path is already costing us [ongoing cost of doing nothing] every [period]. This pays for itself in [timeframe], and avoids [specific future risk].

12 — Explaining technical debt to leadershipSTAKEHOLDER
LEADER

Why do we need to spend time on this instead of new features?

YOU

[System/area] is slowing every new feature down by roughly [X%/time] because of [root cause]. Fixing it now costs [effort]; leaving it means every future feature in this area keeps paying that tax.

13 — "What would you have done differently"INTERVIEW
INTERVIEWER

Looking back, what would you have done differently on that project?

YOU

Knowing what I know now, I'd have [specific change] earlier, because [what it would have prevented]. At the time, we didn't have visibility into [the missing information] — that's actually shaped how I approach [current practice] now.

14 — Scalability decision explanationINTERVIEW
INTERVIEWER

How did you approach scaling [system]?

YOU

We were hitting [specific bottleneck — e.g. p99 latency, DB connections] at [load level]. Rather than over-engineering for hypothetical scale, I targeted the actual bottleneck with [specific fix], which bought us headroom to [new capacity] without a full rearchitecture.

15 — Security vs. speed trade-offDESIGN ROUND
INTERVIEWER

How do you balance shipping fast with security requirements?

YOU

I don't treat it as a binary trade-off — I look at [blast radius / data sensitivity] to decide how much friction is justified. For [low-risk area] we move fast with lighter review; for [high-risk area] we slow down and add [specific control].

16 — Migration strategy explanationSTAKEHOLDER
STAKEHOLDER

How risky is this migration to [new platform]?

YOU

We're doing it in [phases — e.g. strangler pattern] rather than a big-bang cutover, so at every stage we can roll back with [rollback plan]. The riskiest part is [specific step], and we're mitigating that with [mitigation].

17 — Influencing without authorityTEAM
TEAM LEAD

My team doesn't report to you — why should we follow this architecture direction?

YOU

Totally fair to push on that. I'm not asking you to follow it because I said so — here's the problem it solves for [shared pain point], and here's what it would look like if your team piloted it on [low-risk area] first, so you can validate it before committing further.

18 — Vendor/tool selection justificationSTAKEHOLDER
STAKEHOLDER

Why [vendor/tool A] over [vendor/tool B]?

YOU

We evaluated both against [criteria — cost, support, integration effort]. [Tool B] was stronger on [dimension], but [Tool A] won on [the deciding dimension], which mattered more given [our constraint].

19 — Post-incident / postmortem communicationINCIDENT
LEADERSHIP

What happened, and how do we make sure it doesn't happen again?

YOU

Root cause was [specific cause], triggered by [trigger]. Immediate fix was [mitigation], already deployed. The structural fix — [longer-term change] — is scheduled for [timeframe], and closes this specific failure mode.

20 — Closing an interview: your questionsINTERVIEW
INTERVIEWER

Do you have any questions for us?

YOU

Yes — what does the architecture review process actually look like day-to-day here? And what's the biggest architectural trade-off the team is currently navigating with [relevant system/area]?
