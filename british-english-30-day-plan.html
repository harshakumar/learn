Days 1–7 — Foundation

Basic fluency, everyday vocabulary, simple conversations, pronunciation fundamentals, removing hesitation.

Day 1 — Meeting someone for the first time

Warm-up

Say 3×, faster each time: "Red lorry, yellow lorry." · "She sells seashells on the seashore." Then breathe in, sigh out a long "ahhh" ×2 to relax your jaw. Say aloud: "Good morning, how are you?" / "Lovely to meet you." / "What's your name?"

Pronunciation — Non-rhotic R & Schwa /ə/

WordIPAEasy guideSentence
water/ˈwɔːtə/WAW-tuh (no final R)I need some water, please.
car/kɑː/KAH (no R)My car is parked outside.
about/əˈbaʊt/uh-BOWTWhat's this about?

Drill (×5): "The car park is over there" → "the kah pahk is ovuh theh".

Vocabulary — Greetings & first meetings

PhraseMeaningExampleRegister
You alright?Hello, how are youYou alright? Haven't seen you in ages.Informal
How's it going?How are youHow's it going, mate?Informal
CheersThanks / byeCheers for that, see you later.Informal
Lovely to meet youNice to meet youLovely to meet you, I've heard a lot about you.Polite
Fancy a coffee?Would you like a coffeeFancy a coffee before work?Informal

Conversation — First day at work

Tom: Morning! You must be the new starter — I'm Tom.

You: Morning, yes, that's me. Lovely to meet you.

Tom: You alright? First day's always a bit daunting, isn't it?

You: Yeah, a bit! I'm sure I'll settle in though.

Tom: Course you will. Fancy a coffee before we get started?

You: Go on then, that'd be great.

Tom: Brilliant. So, whereabouts are you from? (your turn)

Speaking Challenge

  1. Tell me about your morning so far.
  2. Introduce yourself as if we've just met.
  3. What are you looking forward to this week?
  4. Why do you want to improve your British English?

🇬🇧 British English of the Day

  • You alright? — casual greeting, not literally asking if something's wrong.
  • Cheers — thanks or a casual goodbye.
  • Fancy…? — casual invitation: "Fancy a walk later?"
  • Go on then — casual acceptance.
  • A bit — softener: "It's a bit cold."
❌ I will call you later.
✅ I'll give you a ring later. — both correct; "give you a ring" is more natural everyday British.

Shadowing

"Morning! // You must be the new starter. // Lovely to meet you. // Fancy a coffee before we get started?"
Note the linking: "must be the" flows as one smooth sound — don't pause between words.

🇬🇧 British English of the Day

✅ Could I have the bill, please? — "check" is American; "bill" is British.

Day 29 — 15-minute simulated conversation

Format

Today has no new teaching — it's a test. Set a timer for 15 minutes and speak through the following situations back-to-back, switching roles yourself (you + imaginary other person) with minimal preparation. Don't write anything down first.

Run through, aloud, without stopping to plan:

  1. (2 min) Meet someone new at an event and introduce yourself.
  2. (2 min) Make small talk about the weather and your weekend.
  3. (2 min) Order food and a drink at a café.
  4. (2 min) Ask for and give directions to a nearby landmark.
  5. (2 min) Discuss your job and one work challenge.
  6. (2 min) Give an opinion on a topic and politely disagree with an imaginary counter-view.
  7. (3 min) Handle an unexpected complication — a mix-up with a booking — and resolve it politely.

Pronunciation focus — self-check

While speaking, consciously monitor: non-rhotic R, schwa in unstressed syllables, TH sounds, linking between words, and rising intonation on genuine questions.

Day 30 — Full speaking assessment & next steps

Format — 30-minute comprehensive assessment

This is your final test for the programme. Record the whole session if you can.

  1. (5 min) Warm-up: Free-speak about your experience of the last 30 days — what improved, what was hardest.
  2. (5 min) Pronunciation review: Read aloud five sentences, one for each pronunciation feature you found hardest across the month (choose from: non-rhotic R, TH sounds, glottal stops, schwa, word/sentence stress, intonation, linking, weak forms, vowel length, diphthongs).
  3. (5 min) Vocabulary test: Use 10 phrases from across the 30 days in original sentences, spoken aloud, without looking them up.
  4. (10 min) Fluency & naturalness: Hold an unscripted conversation covering: a recent event in your life, an opinion on a current topic, a story about a mistake (using understatement/humour), and a polite disagreement.
  5. (5 min) Confidence check: Answer 5 unexpected personal questions aloud, with no preparation time:
    • What's a skill you'd like to learn next year?
    • Describe your ideal Saturday.
    • What's a British custom you find interesting or unusual?
    • Tell me about a time you changed your mind about something.
    • What are you most proud of this year?

Common mistakes to review

  • Over-pronouncing R sounds after vowels (car, water) instead of dropping them.
  • Flat intonation on questions and invitations, sounding uninterested.
  • Being too direct with requests and disagreement instead of softening.
  • Forgetting weak forms (of, for, and) and stressing every word equally.
